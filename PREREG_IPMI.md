# IPMI 2027 expansion of "Small regions look homogeneous" - preregistered tests

## TH-A (2026-11-17, written BEFORE running): does the closed-form model predict the plug-in bias with no fitting?
Why: IPMI rule R1 (derive the method from a model). The ISBI version states bias = c/N with c "inflated by correlation" but never predicts c.
Model: stationary Gaussian random field with the exact discrete correlation of the simulation (white noise * Gaussian kernel sigma, wrap); intensities 60 z + 40; fixed bins of width 25 on the absolute scale (first-order entropy and uniformity do not depend on the anchor).
Prediction (theory_bias.py): Cov of bin frequencies in ROI S = (1/N^2) sum_{s,t in S} C_k(t-s), C_k(h) = sum_{n>=1} r(h)^n a_{k,n}^2 / n! (Hermite expansion of bivariate-normal bin indicators). Uniformity bias = sum_k Var(pi_k) (exact). Entropy bias = -(1/(2 ln 2)) sum_k Var(pi_k)/pi_k (second order). Predicted err = bias(ROI) - bias(radius-60 reference), as measured in bench_full.csv.
Target: mean measured err of the plug-in Entropy and Uniformity per (sigma, radius) cell in bench_full.csv (20 ROIs per cell).
GO: across cells with N/xi^3 >= 20 and |measured| > 2 s.e., median relative error |pred - meas| / |meas| <= 0.20 for BOTH features, and Pearson r(pred, meas) >= 0.95 over all 21 cells per feature.
NO-GO: the IPMI version cannot claim a predictive model; keep the empirical ISBI framing (then IPMI slot is weak).
**RESULT TH-A (2026-11-17): NO-GO.** Pearson(pred, meas) 0.98 (Entropy) / 0.98 (Uniformity) passes; median relative error 0.39 / 0.29 fails (bar 0.20).
Two causes, kept apart: (1) the test was underpowered: 20 ROIs per cell give s.e. of 15-45% of the effect; Uniformity, for which the model is exact in expectation, misses mostly within 1-2 s.e. (my error in setting a 20% bar at this noise). (2) Entropy: the second-order expansion over-predicts for small ROIs (-0.30 vs -0.22 +- 0.03 at N=123, xi=1.5), the known Miller-Madow over-correction with under-sampled bins; at larger N it matches (-0.044 vs -0.042).

## TH-A2 (2026-11-17, written BEFORE running, after TH-A): the same prediction, tested with adequate power and against the analytic population value
New simulation (theory_sim.py): 128^3 fields as in bench_synth (same kernel, wrap, 60 z + 40 with the field's own s.d.), 20 fields per sigma, 20 random ROIs per radius per field = 400 ROIs per (sigma, radius) cell; plug-in Entropy and Uniformity with absolute bins of width 25; truth = population value of N(40, 60^2) binned (no reference sphere).
GO for the IPMI 'predictive model' claim: (a) Uniformity: in >= 80% of the cells with N/xi^3 >= 20, |pred - meas| <= max(2 s.e., 0.10 |meas|); (b) Entropy: median relative error <= 0.20 over cells with N/xi^3 >= 20. Both needed. Cells below 20 are reported (theory expected to fail there; that is the paper's identifiability regime).
**RESULT TH-A2 (2026-11-17, 8,400 ROIs, theory_sim.csv/json): NO-GO for the full claim; (a) passes, (b) fails.**
(a) Uniformity: prediction within max(2 s.e., 10%) in 12/12 cells with N/xi^3 >= 20, and also in the small-ROI cells (e.g. xi=6, N=123: 0.1490 vs 0.1493 +- 0.0048). The covariance model is exact for quadratic functionals, with no fitted parameter.
(b) Entropy: the second-order expansion over-predicts by about 40-50% in every cell, also at large N (median relative error 0.49). Likely cause (not tested): the expansion charges every rare tail bin about 1/N even when the ROI almost never occupies it.
Claim allowed in the IPMI version: exact closed-form bias for quadratic features (uniformity, GLCM joint energy) under correlation; the c/N law and its design effect for smooth features; entropy's constant is not predicted by the expansion, which is why the model-free jackknife is needed. No post hoc entropy fix will be claimed without a new preregistered test.

## TH-B (2026-11-17, written BEFORE running): how much information does an ROI carry about the texture's marginal scale when the correlation length is unknown?
Import: fixed-domain (infill) asymptotics in geostatistics (Zhang 2004, JASA 99:250; Stein 1999): for a Matern field only sigma^2 / xi^(2 nu) is consistently estimable within a fixed domain, so sigma (and every marginal feature, e.g. first-order entropy) is not, when the ROI is small relative to xi.
Computation (theory_fisher.py): ROI = sphere of radius 2..8 voxels; field = Matern nu=1/2 (exponential) and nu=3/2, xi in {0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8} voxels, plus mean as nuisance. Efficient Fisher information for log sigma with log xi unknown: I_ss - I_sx^2 / I_xx, from I_ij = 1/2 tr(S^-1 dS_i S^-1 dS_j). Report in units of 'independent-voxel equivalents' (I_eff / 2).
Expectation (GO for using it as Proposition 3 with a figure): (i) for N/xi^3 >= 20, log-log slope of I_eff vs N/xi^3 in [0.85, 1.15]; (ii) for N/xi^3 <= 1, I_eff / 2 <= 5 (the ROI is worth at most ~5 independent voxels for the scale, whatever N). Fails -> the gate is argued only empirically.
**RESULT TH-B (2026-11-17, theory_fisher.json): (ii) PASSES, (i) FAILS -> overall NO-GO as preregistered.**
(ii) For N/xi^3 <= 1 the ROI carries at most 3.9 (nu=1/2) / 4.3 (nu=3/2) independent-voxel equivalents of information about the scale, whatever N (33 to 2109 voxels): the fixed-domain plateau holds.
(i) Pooled log-log slope 0.64 / 0.65, not ~1. Visible cause: for xi < 1 voxel, N/xi^3 exceeds N (more 'independent samples' than voxels); within one xi >= 1 the growth is close to proportional (xi=1: 53 -> 739 for N 123 -> 2109). So N/xi^3 overstates the effective sample size for very rough texture; min(N, N/xi^3)-type indices were not tested and are not claimed.
Allowed claim: Proposition 3 (small-ROI information plateau, after Zhang 2004) with the computed curve; the paper must state that the N/xi^3 index is only a proxy and overstates for xi < 1 voxel.

## GATE-2 (2026-11-19, written BEFORE running): replace the N/xi^3 proxy gate by the model-derived information index
Why: the IPMI draft admits N/xi^3 overstates the effective sample size for xi < 1 voxel, and native CT has median xi = 0.86, i.e. the gate's weak regime is the main real dataset. Prop. 3 already defines the right quantity: I_eff(N, xi) = efficient Fisher information about log sigma with xi unknown, in independent-voxel equivalents (theory_fisher.json, Matern nu = 1/2).
Index: I_eff interpolated in (log xi, log N) from theory_fisher.json rows (nu = 1/2); xi clipped to [0.5, 8]; N beyond the grid (synthetic r = 10, 14) extrapolated with the per-xi log-log fit. Old index: N/xi^3.
Threshold for EACH index tuned on synthetic ground truth only (bench_full.csv, true xi = 2 sigma, 10 fields x 3 sigma x 7 radii, methods plugin / JK): T minimising the objective = mean over the 7 features of RMSE(gated) / RMSE(plugin); grid = the 5..95% quantiles of that index (19 values). Then frozen and applied to real CT (real_all_smooth.csv: 4 smoothing levels, estimated xi per region, as in the paper).
G1 (primary, non-inferiority): on real CT, objective (averaged over the 4 smoothing levels) of the I_eff gate <= that of the N/xi^3 gate + 0.01.
G2 (superiority, secondary): I_eff gate objective <= N/xi^3 gate objective - 0.02.
Readings: G1 -> the paper's gate becomes the model-derived quantity (method fully derived from the model; the 'proxy' limitation is removed; N/xi^3 kept only as an intuition). G1 fails -> keep N/xi^3 with its stated limitation. Script gate2.py, output gate2.json.
**RESULT GATE-2 (2026-11-19, gate2.json): G1 PASS, G2 FAIL.** Thresholds tuned on synthetic: N/xi^3 >= 1.19, I_eff >= 6.0 independent-voxel equivalents (same synthetic objective 0.933). Real CT objective (mean RMSE ratio gated/plug-in over 7 features, mean of 4 smoothing levels): I_eff gate 0.9953 vs N/xi^3 gate 0.9953 (per level .910/.985/1.059/1.026 for both). Paper's N/xi^3 >= 20 gate: 0.980.
Reading (preregistered): the model-derived I_eff gate is non-inferior, so the paper may state the gate as the Prop. 3 quantity (I_eff >= T) and keep N/xi^3 as intuition. Not superior. Also visible (not a criterion): thresholds tuned on synthetic data are far looser than the paper's 20 and make smooth real texture worse (objective > 1 at smoothing 1.4/2.0), so the threshold is the weak point, not the index; the paper keeps the stricter threshold and must say it was not tuned on synthetic data.

## PAY-1 (2026-11-19, written BEFORE running; CPU on the existing RIDER tumour table): does the correction reduce the slice-thickness dependence of tumour texture?
Why: real-data payoff (IPMI rule R4). Reconstructing the same tumour at 5 mm instead of 1.25 mm divides its voxel count by about 4, which by Prop. 1 raises the finite-sample bias; feature disagreement across slice thickness is a known radiomics reproducibility problem. Thickness also changes partial-volume smoothing (true texture change), which no bias correction can remove, so only part of the disagreement is addressable.
Data: ../data/rider/rider_tumour_texture.csv (radiologist segmentations; plug-in, JK and gated values per patient, visit, kernel, thickness). Pairs: same patient, visit and kernel, 1.25 mm vs 5 mm. Features: Entropy, JointEntropy, Uniformity, JointEnergy (the bias-dominated ones; others reported).
D1 (primary): mean |f(5 mm) - f(1.25 mm)|, gated vs plug-in, relative reduction >= 20% for >= 3 of the 4 features, in BOTH kernels.
D2: the reduction is larger for tumours below the median N (at 5 mm) than above, for >= 3 of 4 features (dose-response with size).
Readings: D1 -> payoff sentence: the correction removes a measurable part of slice-thickness dependence of tumour texture. D1 fails -> on these (large) tumours thickness dependence is dominated by real smoothing; no payoff claim, reported as such. Script pay1.py, output pay1.json.
**RESULT PAY-1 (2026-11-19, pay1.json): D1 FAIL, D2 FAIL.** Relative reduction of mean |f(5 mm) - f(1.25 mm)|, gated vs plug-in, Entropy / JointEntropy / Uniformity / JointEnergy: LUNG kernel +.02 / +.08 / +.01 / -.01; STANDARD -.00 / -.06 / -.00 / -.10 (53 / 52 tumour pairs; median N at 5 mm 6436 / 3072 voxels; gate open 92-96%). Only JointEntropy in small tumours (LUNG) +.18.
Reading (preregistered): on these tumours, slice-thickness dependence is dominated by real partial-volume smoothing, not finite-sample bias; no payoff claim. The paper states that the correction does not harmonise slice thickness.
